There is a common trap new CrossFit athletes and coaches fall into: skipping heavy days entirely, or running them wrong.

Let’s be honest—when most people think of CrossFit, they picture breathless, sweaty met-cons. They think of racing the clock, gasping for air on the rower, and collapsing on the floor after a brutal 12-minute AMRAP. But if you think CrossFit is _only_ about high-intensity cardio, you’re missing half the puzzle.

Days devoted to strength training are non-negotiable. They are baked right into the DNA of the methodology.

## Why Heavy Days Matter (The Power Curve)

Heavy days aren’t just about bragging rights or hitting a shiny new personal record on your back squat. They are vital because power output drops the longer you go.

Your absolute work capacity in short, explosive bursts sets the ceiling for your entire fitness curve. Sure, you can be a powerlifter with massive short-duration power and very little endurance elsewhere. But you _cannot_ have high-level long-duration endurance without a solid baseline of short-term power backing it up.

> "Lifting heavy isn’t just about putting a big number on a barbell; it’s about unlocking your body’s absolute potential," says Sian Edwards, owner and founder of CrossFit Warrington. "If you skip the heavy work, you are literally putting a ceiling on your engine. Power output dictates your capacity everywhere else."

## Ditch the Myth: Lifting Heavy is for Everyone

There’s still a lingering, outdated myth that heavy weightlifting is somehow dangerous or unsuitable for women or older adults. Let’s clear that up right now: it’s complete nonsense.

Resistance training and heavy lifting are crucial for preserving bone density, improving metabolic health, and building long-term resilience. For women especially, picking up heavy things completely changes body composition, protects your joints, and shatters the idea that fitness only counts if you're drowning in sweat.

> Sian Edwards shares her perspective from the floor at CrossFit Warrington: "We see so many women walk through our doors who are initially nervous about putting heavy plates on a bar. But the moment they realise what their bodies are truly capable of—whether they are in their twenties or their sixties—everything changes. Weightlifting builds an undeniable physical and mental resilience. Strength is empowering, and it belongs to everyone."

## What a Proper Heavy Day Looks Like

You don't _always_ need a barbell (though let's face it, the barbell is king because nothing else matches its ease of loading). You can build strength with weighted pull-ups, sandbags, or awkward odd objects.

However, when you program a dedicated heavy strength session, the structure needs to make sense:

- Keep the reps low: Think sets of 1 to 5 repetitions.
- Watch the volume: Total working reps should usually sit between 7 and 25.
- Heavy means heavy: Your working sets should hover at 80% to 85% (or more) of your 1-rep max. It should require intense, laser-like focus, but it shouldn't leave you gasping for air like a met-con.

If you do too few reps (like a single sloppy max-effort lift), your muscles don’t get enough time under tension to spark change. If you do too many reps at high loads, you fry your central nervous system and take way too long to recover.

## Coaching and Keeping It Safe

Running a heavy day smoothly in a packed group class takes some finesse. You can't just throw people under a barbell and yell "go."

1. Warm up with a purpose: Get the core temperature up, open up those joints, and refine movement mechanics _before_ the heavy plates go on. Never exhaust your athletes during the warm-up.
2. Teach people how to fail: Every single athlete needs to know how to bail safely out of a missed lift or spot a partner. Never assume they know.
3. Keep your eyes open: The second an athlete's form completely falls apart under a heavy load, drop the weight. Threshold training applies here just as much as anywhere else—mechanics first, always.

## Bring on the Heavy

Whether you’re 18 or 80, fit or brand new to fitness, heavy days belong in your routine. Through the magic of relative intensity, a beginner squatting an empty PVC pipe or a light training bar is getting the exact same neurological and physical stimulus as an experienced athlete hitting a heavy three-rep max.

As Sian and the team at CrossFit Warrington prove every week, making time for strength isn't a detour from CrossFit—it _is_ CrossFit. So stop skipping leg day, load up the bar safely, and watch what your body can actually do.
