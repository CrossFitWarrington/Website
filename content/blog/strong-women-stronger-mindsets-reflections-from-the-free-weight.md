The other week, I found myself in a Globo gym.

Just me, my headphones, and an hour carved out to move my body.

And before anyone says anything — yes, it’s absolutely fine for a gym owner to go and support someone else’s business! In fact, I think it’s healthy to do so. There’s something quite refreshing about walking into a space that isn’t yours, with no responsibilities other than your own workout. No classes to check on, no music volume to adjust, no clients to coach. Just movement, just me.

So there I was, wandering over to the free weights area — you know the bit. The section lined with dumbbells that start at 2.5kg and go all the way up to 50kg, with racks of barbells, benches, and enough mirrors to see yourself from every possible angle.

And I paused.

I looked around — properly looked. And it struck me how different this scene looked compared to what it would have been not so long ago.

Because once upon a time, that corner of the gym was a very male space. It was almost exclusively filled with men: grunting, lifting, spotting each other, and chatting between sets. It was a space that, whether intentionally or not, carried a certain kind of energy — one that made many women feel as though they didn’t belong there.

But this time, what I saw genuinely made me smile.

Around the benches and dumbbell racks were women. Lots of them. Women of all ages — some younger girls just starting out, still learning their form; some middle-aged women confidently moving through their workouts; and a few older women, strong and steady, showing that strength has absolutely no age limit.

And in that moment, I felt genuinely proud.

Proud of how far we’ve come as women in fitness. Proud that we’ve pushed past the old stereotypes — that weights are “for men” and cardio is “for women.” Proud that we now understand that strength training isn’t about becoming bulky or masculine; it’s about health, vitality, confidence, and capability.

## Why Strength Training Matters So Much for Women

There’s been such a positive shift recently in how we view fitness. For years, women were told to stick to the treadmill or the light weights if they didn’t want to “get too big.” Thankfully, that outdated mindset is being replaced by science, education, and real-life examples of strong women who are changing the narrative.

Strength training has so many benefits for women of every age:

- It builds lean muscle, boosts metabolism and supports healthy body composition.
- It improves posture, balance and coordination.
- It enhances bone density, helping to protect against osteoporosis.
- It reduces the risk of injury and supports joint health.
- It boosts mental wellbeing, reducing stress and improving mood.
- And it gives us that incredible sense of achievement when you lift something you once thought you couldn’t.

For older women especially, lifting weights can be life-changing. As we age, our bodies naturally lose muscle mass and bone density — but strength training helps slow, and even reverse, those effects. It keeps us strong, independent and mobile. It supports heart health and cognitive function. And perhaps most importantly, it reinforces the message that being strong and capable isn’t limited to a particular decade of life.

## A Cultural Shift Worth Celebrating

What I witnessed that day wasn’t just a few women lifting weights — it was a reflection of a broader cultural shift.

Women are no longer shying away from the barbell. We’re no longer standing on the sidelines of the weights area, waiting for “our turn.” We’re claiming our space, quite literally, and saying: we belong here too.

And what’s beautiful is that it’s not about competing with men or proving a point. It’s about owning our health, our strength, and our identity. It’s about training for life — to carry our shopping, to play with our kids, to feel powerful in our own skin.

It’s also about the next generation.

I look at younger girls now — the ones in their teens and twenties — and I’m honestly inspired. They’re so much more informed, more curious, more willing to challenge the old norms. They do their research. They know the benefits. And they’re not letting fear or judgment keep them out of the spaces they want to be in.

To them, the free weights area isn’t intimidating — it’s just another part of the gym. As it should be.

## A Note to All the Women Out There

So, to every woman who’s ever hesitated to pick up a dumbbell — this is your sign to do it.

To the ones who’ve already started — keep going. You’re setting the standard for what’s possible.

And to the next generation — thank you. You’re changing the narrative, one rep at a time.

Because strength training isn’t just about building muscle — it’s about building confidence, resilience, and community. It’s about rewriting what “feminine strength” looks like.

That day in the Globo gym, standing there amongst those racks and mirrors, I felt a genuine sense of gratitude and pride.

Gratitude for the women who paved the way.

Pride in the women who are showing up today.

And excitement for the women who are yet to discover just how powerful they can be.

So here’s to all of you — the women who lift, the ones who are learning to, and the ones thinking about giving it a go.

Keep showing up. Keep getting stronger. Keep proving that strength belongs to all of us.

Because this space — every single dumbbell, barbell and rack — isn’t just for men anymore. It’s for everyone.

And that’s something truly worth celebrating. 💪
