Protein is often the headline nutrient in fitness and nutrition conversations—and for good reason. Whether your goal is fat loss, muscle gain, or simply improving your health, protein plays a central role in how your body functions and adapts.

## What Is Protein and Why Is It Important?

Protein is one of the three macronutrients (alongside carbohydrates and fats). It’s made up of amino acids, which your body uses to:

- Repair and build muscle tissue
- Support immune function
- Produce enzymes and hormones
- Maintain healthy skin, hair, and nails

Unlike fats and carbohydrates, protein isn’t stored in large amounts, so regular intake is essential.

According to the NHS, protein should be a key part of a balanced, healthy diet.

## Protein and Weight Loss

If your goal is fat loss, protein is one of your biggest allies.

### Keeps You Fuller for Longer

Protein helps control hunger hormones, making it easier to stick to a calorie deficit without constant cravings.

### Preserves Lean Muscle

When dieting, the aim is to lose fat—not muscle. Higher protein intake helps maintain lean tissue, keeping your metabolism stronger.

### Higher Calorie Burn

Your body uses more energy to digest protein compared to carbs and fats, supporting overall calorie expenditure.

The British Nutrition Foundation highlights protein’s role in maintaining muscle during weight loss phases.

## Protein and Performance

For anyone training regularly, protein is non-negotiable.

### Muscle Growth & Repair

Training creates micro-tears in muscle fibres. Protein repairs and rebuilds them stronger.

### Faster Recovery

Adequate protein reduces soreness and helps you bounce back quicker between sessions.

### Better Results

Without enough protein, progress in strength, endurance, and body composition is limited.

## How Much Protein Do You Need?

Guidelines depend on your goal:

- **General health:** ~0.75g per kg body weight
- **Fat loss:** 1.2–1.6g per kg
- **Performance/muscle building:** 1.6–2.2g per kg

**Example:**  
A 75kg individual aiming for fat loss may target 90–120g daily.

## Best Protein Sources

### Animal-Based

- Chicken, turkey
- Eggs
- Greek yoghurt
- Fish (salmon, tuna)
- Lean beef

### Plant-Based

- Lentils
- Chickpeas
- Tofu/tempeh
- Beans
- Quinoa

## Practical Guidelines for Results

- Include protein in every meal (20–40g)
- Prioritise whole foods
- Eat protein after training
- Stay consistent daily
- Balance with carbs and fats

## Where Most People Go Wrong

- Not eating enough protein when dieting
- Relying too heavily on shakes
- Ignoring overall calorie intake
